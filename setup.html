Cyber Safety Code of Conduct

Safety is our absolute priority. Please review these rules with your Scout before they participate in any online communication channels.

  • No Personal Details: Never share your full name, home address, school name, phone number, or social media handles with anyone on chat, even if they claim to be a Scout.
  • Use Only First Names: Always use your first name or your assigned Scout nickname when conversing on international channels.
  • Stay in Supervised Areas: Only use the official links provided in the dashboard. Do not follow links sent via private message.
  • Report Concerning Behavior: If a conversation makes you feel uncomfortable, or someone asks inappropriate questions, close the tab immediately and inform your Parent or Scout Leader.
  • Be Respectful: Remember the Scout Law. Be polite, friendly, and respectful to all international participants. Bullying or inappropriate language will result in an immediate global ban.

Parent Glossary

Scouting acronyms can be confusing. Here is a brief guide to the terms your Scout might use over the weekend.

JOTA stands for Jamboree on the Air. It is an annual event in which Scouts and Guides all over the world speak to each other by means of amateur radio contacts. Local ham radio operators volunteer their time and equipment to make this possible.

JOTI stands for Jamboree on the Internet. It runs parallel to JOTA and connects Scouts globally via text chat, voice chat, video conferencing, and multiplayer games hosted on secure, moderated servers.

ScoutLink is a global, non-profit network providing safe, supervised chat environments for Scouts and Guides. They host the primary IRC (Internet Relay Chat) servers used during the JOTI weekend.

QSO is an amateur radio term meaning a conversation or contact between two operators. During JOTA, Scouts will attempt to log as many QSOs as possible with other groups around the world.
