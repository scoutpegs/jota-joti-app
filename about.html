About Boulder Scout Group

Boulder Scout Group is based at Boulder Scout Hall, 10 Hamilton Street South, Boulder WA 6432. This site provides information about the group’s four youth sections, its public calendar, how to arrange a first visit and official Scouting resources.

The history page labels the evidence behind each milestone. Some early dates and details are group-supplied accounts awaiting confirmation from original records; they should not be presented as independently verified facts.

Contact the group at gl.boulder@scoutswa.com.au.

Our history

A Goldfields Scout tradition shaped by outdoor learning, community service and a much-loved hall. Dates are labelled according to the evidence available.

Foundation in the Goldfields

The group’s historical account places Boulder Scout Group’s establishment in 1908, during the earliest years of Australian Scouting. It describes the group as giving local young people opportunities for outdoor adventure, practical skills and service. The exact Boulder founding date remains to be confirmed by an original local record.

1944
Group-supplied account

Community service during wartime

The group’s account says the troop was asked to march and stand guard at the November 1944 funeral of Sophia Mabel Hopkins, following a request from the Acting District Commissioner. This specific funeral detail is awaiting confirmation from an original newspaper notice, troop record or funeral programme.

Trove research starting pointCity archives for further verification
1946
Group-supplied account

A hall made from a split aircraft hangar

According to the group’s account, a former World War II aircraft hangar was divided into two sections after the war. One half reportedly went to Campbell Street for Kalgoorlie Scouts; the other became Boulder Scout Group’s hall on Hamilton Street. The engineering and transport story should be treated as oral history until a district, council or building record is located.

City archives for further verification
2018
Published source

Local recognition for youth-service volunteering

The Kalgoorlie-Boulder Volunteer Awards listed Brenda Clark of 1st Boulder Scout Group as the individual Youth category award recipient in December 2018. The report provides a published record of Scouting-related youth service being recognised in the local community.

Kalgoorlie Miner report · 10 December 2018
2019
National Scouting milestone

A renewed youth program

Scouts Australia launched its new youth program at AJ2019, with implementation beginning in April 2019. The national change was built around a continuous “One Program, One Journey” approach. This is movement-wide context, not a claim about Boulder’s exact local implementation date.

Scouts Australia · 2019 youth program
2019
Published source

Cubs care for the Kalgoorlie Arboretum

Boulder Cub Scouts took part in a clean-up at the Kalgoorlie Arboretum, putting community service and care for the local environment into practice.

Kalgoorlie Miner report
2020
Published source

Keeping Scouting connected during COVID-19

During pandemic restrictions, Boulder Scout Group continued connecting through Zoom catch-ups, helping the group stay prepared while normal in-person meetings were disrupted.

Kalgoorlie Miner report · 22 May 2020
2021–22
Published source

Speaking up for local community space

The group publicly questioned a decision about the future use of a former public building behind the Scout hall, highlighting the importance of keeping community facilities available to local youth and not-for-profit groups.

Kalgoorlie Miner report
2022
Published source

Community support for lasting traditions

The Rotary Club of Boulder contributed funding towards new section flags and signage, and helped with the cost of restumping the historic hall on Hamilton Street. The report records $2,500 for equipment/signage and $1,000 towards restumping support.

Kalgoorlie Miner report · 1 September 2022
2023
Published source

A $100,000 hall-restoration fundraising milestone

Boulder Scout Group reached its reported $100,000 fundraising goal to help restump the ageing hall, a major step towards securing the building for future generations.

Kalgoorlie Miner report
2024
Published source

Hall restoration and reopening

The hall’s major restoration project culminated in a reopening celebrated by local Scout members and leaders. The group-supplied account describes a “Sponsor-a-Stump” campaign, 108 replacement stumps and six foundation beams, with the hall reopening on 23 June 2024; the exact construction quantities and date are retained as group-supplied details pending an original project report.

Kalgoorlie Miner hall reopening report

Group-supplied account The group also reports that Boulder leaders helped mentor and relaunch Kalgoorlie Scout Group in February 2024 after a three-year pause, as local membership grew.

2025
Published source

On the national stage at AJ2025

The 26th Australian Scout Jamboree brought thousands of Scouts together in Queensland. Boulder’s account records a contingent of 10 youth and two leaders after local fundraising; those exact Boulder numbers remain group-supplied, while the national event itself is documented by Scouts Australia.

Scouts Australia · AJ2025
2026
Published source

Recognition for service to young people

Scouts Australia’s 2026 Adult Recognition Awards listing names Shane Treen as Assistant Scout Leader at Boulder Scout Group. The listing confirms his role in the group but does not specify the award level beside his name.

Scouts Australia · 2026 awards listing

Visit and contact

Boulder Scout Group meets at Boulder Scout Hall, 10 Hamilton Street South, Boulder WA 6432, Australia, and welcomes families from Boulder and Kalgoorlie-Boulder. Email gl.boulder@scoutswa.com.au before a first visit; families can try three weeks free. Age sections: Joey Scouts 5 to 8, Cub Scouts 8 to 11, Scouts 11 to 14, Venturer Scouts 15 to 18. The only regular meeting time published is Venturer Scouts on Wednesdays, 6 to 8 pm; confirm all other dates on the calendar or with the group.
